HealthVision

Range of motion, measured on a video call.

HealthVision began as a digital goniometer for telehealth patients. It uses the phone's camera and motion sensors to guide the patient through standardized movements, measures joint angles in real time, and builds an objective record a clinician can follow from visit to visit.

It was built after physiatrists described how hard it is to examine a joint accurately over telemedicine. HealthVision quantifies, logs and graphs each patient's movement so that a remote visit can include the objective measurement usually taken by hand in the room.

How a measurement is made

From a phone camera to a number a clinician can chart, in five steps.

  1. Capture

    The patient's own phone records a guided, standardized movement. No added hardware.

  2. Landmark detection

    Computer vision locates the joint centers in every frame, with motion sensors for orientation.

  3. Error correction

    An AI layer checks each measurement before it is accepted.

  4. Angle computation

    Joint angles are computed from the landmark geometry in real time.

  5. Clinician dashboard

    Every reading is logged and trended across visits for the care team.

Multilingual intake assistant

The patient answers in Spanish. The physician reads the history in English.

Patients with limited English often cannot describe pain with the precision a diagnosis depends on: tingling, burning, radiating. The assistant takes the history before the visit, in the patient's language, follows up where an answer is incomplete, and delivers a structured summary to the physician.

Currently being clinically piloted with Spanish-speaking patients.

Facial nerve assessment

Developed in research collaboration with Weill Cornell Medicine, Otolaryngology and Head and Neck Surgery.

Recovery after parotid, skull base and head and neck surgery is still graded largely by eye, and grades vary between examiners. This platform tracks facial landmarks while the patient performs standard movements and reports twelve micro-measurements across six nerve territories: symmetry and amplitude indices for each territory, a composite score, and a localization map for the surgeon. It stores landmark coordinates, never raw video.
